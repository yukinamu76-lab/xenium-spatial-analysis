**Xenium sample preparation and spatial transcriptomics**

Axillary lymph nodes were harvested from PBS- or albumin-treated 4T1-bearing mice on day 10 after 4T1 transplantation. Tissues were fixed in 10% neutral buffered formalin (FUJIFILM Wako Pure Chemical Corporation) for 48 h and subsequently transferred to 70% ethanol (nacalai tesque). Samples were submitted to the Division of Histological Study, Center for Anatomical Studies, Graduate School of Medicine, Kyoto University, for formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded (FFPE) block preparation. FFPE blocks were sectioned at a thickness of 5 μm, and tissue sections were mounted onto Xenium slides (10x Genomics).

Xenium in situ gene-expression profiling was performed using the Xenium Prime 5K Mouse Pan Tissue & Pathways Assay (10x Genomics) according to the Xenium Prime In Situ Gene Expression with optional Cell Segmentation Staining User Guide (CG000760, Rev C, 10x Genomics). The predesigned Xenium Prime 5K Mouse Pan Tissue & Pathways panel, comprising 5,006 RNA targets, was supplemented with a custom add-on panel of 47 genes, resulting in a total of 5,053 RNA targets (Table EV9). Tissue sections underwent deparaffinization and decrosslinking, followed by priming hybridization, RNase treatment, and polishing according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Gene-specific probes from the predesigned and custom panels were subsequently hybridized to the tissue sections at 50°C for 18 h. Following post-hybridization washing, bound probes were ligated and subjected to rolling-circle amplification to generate amplified gene-specific barcode products.

Following amplification, cell-segmentation staining was performed using the Xenium Multi-Tissue Stain workflow, followed by chemical quenching of tissue autofluorescence and nuclear staining with DAPI. Prepared slides were loaded onto the Xenium Analyzer (10x Genomics), and regions of interest encompassing the lymph node tissues were selected for analysis. Iterative cycles of fluorescent probe hybridization, imaging, and signal decoding were then performed automatically by the Xenium Analyzer to determine the spatial coordinates and gene identities of individual RNA transcripts. Data acquisition was performed using Xenium instrument software version 4.0.1.4, and primary Xenium data processing was performed using Xenium analysis software version 4.0.1.0. Cell segmentation incorporated boundary staining (ATP1A1/CD45/E-Cadherin), interior RNA staining (18S), and 5.0-μm nuclear expansion.



**Xenium data processing and cell-type annotation**

Xenium In Situ output data were analyzed in R (version 4.6.0) using Seurat (version 5.5.0). Cell-by-gene expression matrices were imported using LoadXenium, and cell-centroid coordinates were obtained from the Xenium cells.parquet files (with cells.csv.gz used as a fallback). To retain cell populations with relatively low transcript abundance, particularly neutrophils, a permissive quality-control threshold was applied, and cells with more than three detected genes (nFeature_Xenium > 3) were retained. The retained cells were merged across the lymph nodes from the PBS- and albumin-treated mouse and normalized using NormalizeData with log normalization.

Because of the large number of cells, dimensionality reduction and clustering were performed using Seurat's sketch-based workflow. Highly variable features were identified using FindVariableFeatures, and a 50,000-cell sketch was generated by leverage-score sampling using SketchData. Within the sketch assay, variable features were re-identified, expression values were scaled using ScaleData, and principal component analysis was performed using RunPCA, with 50 principal components calculated. Based on inspection of the variance-explained elbow plot, the first 20 components were used for downstream analyses. Sample-level integration was performed in the sketch using Harmony (IntegrateLayers with HarmonyIntegration, grouped by orig.ident). A shared nearest-neighbor graph was constructed from the first 20 Harmony dimensions using FindNeighbors, and clustering was performed using FindClusters at a resolution of 0.5, yielding 16 clusters. A two-dimensional UMAP embedding was generated for the sketch from the Harmony representation. The integrated reduction and cluster labels were then projected to the full Xenium dataset using ProjectIntegration and ProjectData, followed by generation of a full-dataset UMAP from the projected Harmony representation.

Cell types were assigned manually on the basis of canonical marker-gene expression, guided in part by a previously published cell-type marker set (Vandenbon *et al.*, 2023), together with cluster-enriched genes. Cluster-enriched genes were identified on the 50,000-cell sketch using FindAllMarkers with a Wilcoxon rank-sum test (only.pos = TRUE, min.pct = 0.25, and logfc.threshold = 0.25). Genes were ranked within each cluster by average log2 fold change, and the top 10 marker genes were used to support cell-type assignments; the top three enriched markers per cluster were additionally visualized using DotPlot. The 16 clusters were annotated into major immune and stromal cell populations, including CD8+ T cells. Cell-type annotations, cluster-specific top 10 marker genes, and the numbers and proportions of cells in each cluster are provided in Table EV10.



**CD8+ T-cell transcriptional-state analysis**

To characterize CD8+ T-cell functional states, normalized expression of immune checkpoint-associated genes (Pdcd1, Ctla4, Havcr2, Lag3, and Tigit) and genes associated with progenitor/exhaustion-related states (Tcf7, Entpd1, Cxcl13, and Tox) was examined within the annotated CD8+ T-cell population. For each gene, normalized expression values were standardized to z scores across all annotated CD8+ T cells from the lymph nodes of the PBS- and albumin-treated mouse combined. The resulting single-cell z scores were then averaged separately within each treatment condition. Thus, standardization was performed before group-wise averaging. Group-mean z scores and the albumin-minus-PBS difference were calculated and visualized as a heatmap. Cell-level PBS-versus-albumin comparisons were additionally calculated using Wilcoxon rank-sum tests with Benjamini-Hochberg correction and retained in the analysis output.



**Operational definition of PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils and exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cells**

For spatial analyses, PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils were operationally defined independently of transcriptional cluster assignment as cells containing at least one Itgam transcript and at least one Ly6g transcript localized within the nuclear boundary, as indicated by overlaps_nucleus = 1 in the Xenium transcript output. Exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cells were operationally defined among annotated CD8+ T cells as those containing at least one nuclear-localized Pdcd1 transcript together with at least one nuclear-localized transcript of Havcr2, Lag3, or Tigit. No transcript QV cutoff was applied in the primary analysis (QV_MIN = 0); a QV >= 20 threshold was evaluated as a sensitivity analysis.



**Spatial association analysis of PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils and CD8+ T cells**

The primary spatial association analysis focused on the lymph node from the PBS-treated mouse because the lymph node from the albumin-treated mouse contained too few PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils for robust spatial analysis. Spatial regions were delineated from cell-centroid x-y coordinates using density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (DBSCAN; eps = 50 um, minPts = 100). DBSCAN clusters containing fewer than 5,000 cells were excluded from region-level analyses.

Among the three retained regions in the lymph node from the PBS-treated mouse, cluster 3 was selected as the primary region for spatial association analysis because it formed a contiguous tissue region and contained 134,639 cells, including 11 PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils and 32,978 CD8+ T cells, of which 128 were classified as exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cells. Cluster 1 contained only three PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils, whereas cluster 2 had an irregular and fragmented geometry. Logistic regression analyses of clusters 1 and 2 were nevertheless retained as supplementary analyses. 

For each retained region containing at least one PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil, the Euclidean distance from each CD8+ T cell to its nearest PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil was calculated using nearest-neighbor search implemented in the RANN package. Logistic regression with a binomial link was used to model the probability that a CD8+ T cell exhibited the exhaustion-associated phenotype as a function of distance to the nearest PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil. Distance was scaled in units of 100 um, and associations were summarized as odds ratios per 100-um increase in distance with 95% Wald confidence intervals and Wald *P* values.

To assess whether the observed distance coefficient could arise by chance given the fixed CD8+ T-cell positions and distances, exhaustion-associated labels were randomly reassigned among CD8+ T cells 1,000 times while preserving the observed number of exhaustion-associated cells. The logistic regression model was refitted after each permutation to generate a null distribution of the distance coefficient (beta). Empirical one- and two-sided *P* values were calculated as the proportions of permuted coefficients at least as extreme as the observed coefficient in the corresponding direction or in absolute magnitude, respectively.

As a complementary nearest-neighbor analysis, the Euclidean distance from each PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil to its nearest exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cell was calculated within each lymph node region and visualized using violin plots. Thus, logistic regression evaluated the distance from each CD8+ T cell to its nearest PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil, whereas the complementary analysis evaluated the reciprocal distance from each PMN-MDSC-like neutrophil to its nearest exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cell. Spatial distributions of PMN-MDSC-like neutrophils, exhaustion-associated CD8+ T cells, and other CD8+ T cells were also visualized. Region areas were approximated from the convex hull of cell-centroid coordinates; an additional occupied-area/convex-hull metric based on a 50-um grid was calculated as a geometry descriptor. Region-level areas, cellular composition, and cell counts were summarized in Table EV11.
